# Field brief: closed-loop self-improvement of LLM agents against a proper score, with a human commit gate

**Date:** 2026-08-30 · **Prepared for:** designing `fund`'s tier-2 improvement loop — a nightly seat that reads the calibration scoreboard and reflections and proposes changes to other seats' charters, config numbers, gate thresholds, and the watchlist, projected to a PR the human merges or refuses; the firm never applies its own proposal · **Depth:** deep, 9 searches, 21 sources read (5 PDFs read page-by-page after the fetch paraphrase failed or inverted the finding) · **Extends:** `field-brief-agent-improvement-loops.md` (2026-08-18), which covered trace review, LLM-hacking base rates, eval overfitting, and reflective-memory confabulation and is not repeated here. Claims log with per-claim status: `claims-log-self-improving-agents.md`.

## State of the field *(prose)*

Automatic prompt optimisation has, in the last year, moved from "search the prompt space against a score" (OPRO, MIPROv2) to "read the traces and edit what failed" — GEPA's reflective mutation reads the program trajectory and the evaluator's textual feedback, ETGPO builds an error taxonomy from failed traces and writes guidance only for the prevalent categories, and Agentic Harness Engineering (AHE) evolves a whole agent harness by distilling millions of trace tokens into a layered evidence corpus. The three agree on the input (traces plus feedback, never the score alone) and on the output discipline (small, targeted, attributable edits), and two of them report the unglamorous finding that matters most for an operator: most of the budget goes to *validation*, not generation, and when you skimp on it the optimiser overfits — Decagon watched GEPA grow prompts 75% longer and get *worse* when fed 500 examples instead of 50. The sharpest 2026 result is AHE's: every edit ships with a self-declared prediction of what it will fix and what it might break, the next round grades the prediction, and the loop turns out to be decent at predicting fixes (precision 34%, ~5× chance) and nearly blind to regressions (recall 11%, ~2× chance). In the same paper the system-prompt-only variant *regresses* below the seed while tools, middleware and memory transfer across benchmarks and model families — a finding an enterprise team at Zup reached independently by hand: tool descriptions, schemas and error contracts moved reliability more consistently than prompt engineering.

On the scoring side nothing has changed since Gneiting & Raftery, but the incentive literature is now explicit that a proper rule is not enough once forecasters are *ranked* or *self-select*: under average Brier with question choice a forecaster should abstain on anything between her current score and its complement, and any rank-based reward induces extremising. Both problems have known fixes — score the sum not the mean, impute abstentions, use lotteries — and `specs/calibration.md` already applies the first two. What the scoring literature does not cover, and what this brief had to assemble, is the new attack surface: an optimiser that edits the *forecaster's instructions* to move the score. The closest prior art is AHE's controllability rule (the evolve agent cannot touch the verifier, the model, or the budget) and the AlphaEvolve interview study, where eight mathematicians describe specification gaming as routine and the human's job as tightening the spec each round.

The human gate, finally, has data behind it that did not exist a year ago: across 400 repeat reviewers and 11,429 reviews of agent-authored PRs, approval rose 14.5 points with exposure while change-requests halved and comment effort fell 22%, in the same repositories where approval of *human* PRs was falling. A second dataset (932k PRs) shows agent PRs get human-only review 8% of the time versus 25% for human PRs. The gate decays by default; the countermeasures in the literature are the ones this brief carries into the design.

## Core concepts and vocabulary

- **Reflective mutation** — a proposal step that reads execution traces and textual evaluator feedback, not just a score (GEPA §3). The current default; score-only proposal (OPRO-style) is now the baseline everyone beats.
- **Pareto candidate pool** — keep candidates that win on *some* subset of examples rather than only the best average (GEPA); the guard against a single over-fitted winner.
- **Error taxonomy** — a categorisation of failed traces by failure mode, with prevalence counts; ETGPO derives it with an LLM and filters categories seen in only one problem. Contested whether an LLM may derive it (see debates).
- **Change manifest** — AHE's per-edit record: failure evidence → inferred root cause → targeted fix → predicted impact (expected fixes + at-risk regressions) → next-round verdict. The most complete proposal schema found.
- **Decision observability** — AHE's term for making every edit a falsifiable prediction that the next evaluation confirms or reverts. Distinct from *component* observability (one file per editable surface, one commit per edit) and *experience* observability (the distilled evidence corpus).
- **Controllability** — the optimiser's own write surface is fenced: verifier, model, budget, and seed prompt are read-only or non-deletable (AHE §3.3). The self-modification analogue of `fund`'s invariant 2.
- **Length regularisation** — a character cap inside the reflection prompt; ~4× compression at ~0.8% cost in Decagon's production run. Prompt length is treated as a symptom of overfitting.
- **Tight-budget regime** — RoboPhD's framing: when evaluations are the scarce resource, a validation split buys 7–13 candidates per 1,500 evaluations; fresh random samples plus rating accumulation across rounds is the alternative.
- **Regression blindness** — AHE's measured asymmetry: a self-evolving loop predicts its fixes far better than its breakages.
- **Question self-selection / abstention incentive** — under average Brier, abstain when the true probability is in (b, 1−b) (Alignment Problems, Thm 1). Killed by scoring the sum and imputing abstentions — which `calibration.md` §0.2–0.3 already do.
- **Rank distortion** — competing for rank under a proper rule induces extremising; the ELF lottery mechanism restores truthfulness.
- **Standardised Brier** — GJP's within-question standardisation so that self-selected easy questions do not flatter a forecaster (Mellers 2015).
- **Habituation at the gate** — rising approval and falling scrutiny of agent PRs with reviewer exposure, not explained by PR size or calendar; the measured form of automation bias in code review.
- **Streak audit** — flag a run of consecutive approvals for secondary review; one of three countermeasures the habituation authors propose.

## Live debates and open questions *(prose)*

**Who derives the failure taxonomy.** The practitioner line carried into the 2026-08-18 brief (Husain & Shankar) says never automate open coding of raw traces: reading them is how the operator acquires the judgment the automation is meant to scale, and the taxonomy needs one named human owner. ETGPO (Singh, Yadav, Blanco — Arizona/ServiceNow) derives the taxonomy with an LLM in batches, filters categories that occur in only one problem, and matches or beats GEPA and MIPROv2 on held-out sets at a third of the optimisation tokens; AHE (Fudan/Peking/Qiji) goes further and has an agent distil the evidence corpus with no human in the loop at all. These are not the same question — Husain & Shankar are talking about what the *operator* learns, the papers about what the *optimiser* needs — but a design has to pick. The prior design picked the human line and made it a hard trigger (≥100 traces read by hand before any Class C loop); nothing read here refutes that for a firm that generates 10–25 traces a day and can read all of them, and ETGPO's singleton filter is worth adopting whichever side derives the categories.

**Validate offline, or accumulate online.** GEPA and *When Generic Prompt Improvements Hurt* both spend most of the budget on held-out validation and treat any gain not confirmed there as noise; the latter puts numbers on it — golden sets of 50–200 for iteration, 400–600 paired examples to detect a 5-point absolute difference at 95%. RoboPhD argues the opposite for tight budgets: at 1,500 evaluations a validation split leaves you 7–13 candidates, so it uses fresh random samples every round, accepts that at n=20 the better candidate wins only 45% of comparisons, and lets an Elo rating accumulate the weak signal across ~21 rounds. For a fund whose graded calls arrive at a few per day, the second regime is the honest one: no single proposal will clear an offline significance bar, and the design has to grade the *proposer* on accumulated prediction accuracy rather than grade each proposal on its own delta.

**Does prose transfer at all.** AHE's ablation is stark — swap only the evolved system prompt into the seed and pass@1 *falls* from 69.7% to 67.4%, while memory, tools and middleware each lift it and the frozen harness transfers across benchmarks and model families; the authors read it as "factual harness structure transfers … whereas prose-level strategy does not," and Zup's team found the same by hand. ETGPO and GEPA show the other half: prose guidance scoped to prevalent, trace-derived error categories does generalise to held-out sets, and GEPA's evolved prompts are shorter and generalise better than MIPROv2's. The reconciliation is an inference, not a settled result: prose transfers when it is short, targeted at an observed failure category, and carries examples (ETGPO's short-guidance ablation costs 2.2 points), and it regresses when it is generic strategy. That is the argument for starting tier 2 on config and contracts and admitting charter prose last.

## Practitioner heuristics *(prose)*

People who run these loops well treat the proposal as a forecast, not a patch. The edit names the failure it saw, the cause it inferred, what it expects to move and what it might break — and it is judged next round on whether that happened, not on whether the rationale read well. They keep edits small enough that attribution is a single number, which is why the same teams that automate the loop fence what it may touch: the verifier, the model, the budget, and the seed prompt are off the table, not because the optimiser is malicious but because those are the first things a score-maximiser reaches for. They are stingy with prompt prose. When a prompt does need to change they scope it to one prevalent error category, filter out anything seen once, cap the length, and keep examples in; a prompt that grows is treated as a prompt that is overfitting. They spend most of their evaluation budget checking rather than generating, and when the budget is too small for that — which it always is for a system graded on horizon-resolved outcomes at a few per day — they stop trying to prove any single change and instead track the proposer's hit rate across many changes, the way a tournament separates skill from luck only after a hundred questions. On the human side they design for the gate to decay: one proposal at a time, refuse anything large, show the approver their own approval streak next to what those approvals later did, and audit runs of consecutive yeses. And they measure the thing the vision is tempted to celebrate: an approval rate that climbs while comments fall is the habituation signature, not a sign the firm got good.

## What this means for `fund` (grounded overseer notes)

- **The proposal schema exists; borrow AHE's manifest.** `submit_proposal` should carry: evidence rows (resolution ids, scorecard rows, reflection ids), the inferred cause, the targeted change, the predicted metric and direction, *and a declared set of at-risk metrics that must not move* — abstention rate, confidence granularity, coverage — the three levers a charter edit can pull to flatter Brier. AHE's numbers say the proposer will be poor at naming regressions on its own; the schema forces the question.
- **Calibration.md already closes the classic vectors** (abstain = 0.5, rank by total, shrinkage), so the residual attack is the optimiser editing the seat. The mitigation is structural, per AHE §3.3 and invariant 2's pattern: the Proposer's tool surface cannot reach `calibration/`, `gate/`, or its own charter; its targets are an enumerated allow-list; and a charter proposal must leave the seat's output contract and confidence-step instructions untouched (those are what Alignment Problems' Theorem 1 attacks through).
- **Grade the proposer, not the proposal.** At 10–25 graded calls/day across two desks, a single charter change cannot clear Luck-vs-Skill's 100-question 1σ bar inside a month; a fund-wide threshold change might, a per-seat one will not. So: every proposal is a prediction with a horizon; resolve it as the DiD delta the 2026-08-18 design already specifies (changed seat vs unchanged seats over the same window); and the proposer's *record* — precision of its fix predictions, its regression blind-spot rate — is the scoreboard row, accumulated Elo-style. One proposal per night is the right cadence for attribution, and the wrong one for statistical power; the prior design's *monthly, ≤1/seat* is the honest rate until volume grows.
- **Sequence tier 2 by attribution cost, which the evidence says is also transfer risk:** thresholds and seat config (one number) → watchlist and tool/output-contract wording (Zup's most reliable surface) → charter prose, only as category-scoped guidance once a taxonomy exists (the prior design's trigger, which nothing here relaxes). The AHE system-prompt-only regression is the single strongest reason not to start with charters.
- **Design the gate for decay.** The habituation study's three countermeasures map directly: (1) the morning proposal post shows Benjamin's own approval/refusal trajectory beside what merged proposals later did to the metric; (2) three consecutive merges trigger a "read this one twice" flag; (3) the proposal PR is refused by code if it touches more than one target. VISION's success number (proposals-originated vs human-written) needs a companion: approval rate and change-request rate on proposals, monitored, not maximised.
- **Keep the human-read-traces trigger.** The taxonomy debate is live, but the firm's volume is readable and the practitioner argument is about what the operator learns; adopt ETGPO's singleton filter regardless of who codes the categories.

## Coverage edges *(prose)*

Nothing read here evaluates a proposal loop against *horizon-resolved financial outcomes*; every optimiser result is on annotation, reasoning, or coding benchmarks where the label arrives at evaluation time, and the transfer of their sample-size guidance to five-day-horizon Brier is arithmetic, not evidence. The BSS sampling-variance literature (Bröcker & Smith) and the automation-bias base literature (Parasuraman & Manzey) could not be fetched and are carried as search-level and prior-knowledge respectively — the habituation and oversight-design papers stand in for the latter. No source was found on a human approving *config* changes proposed by an agent in a running system (Zup and Intercom are code PRs; AHE runs unattended), so the tier-2 sequencing recommendation is an inference from adjacent findings. The ETGPO extraction via fetch was unreliable and its numbers were taken from the PDF directly; the Data-Prompt Co-Evolution and Design Considerations papers were read only through paraphrase and are cited for direction, never for figures.
